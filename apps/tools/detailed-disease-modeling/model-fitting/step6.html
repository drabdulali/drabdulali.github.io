6 MLE Optimization

Maximum Likelihood Estimation

Find \(\hat\beta\) and \(\hat\gamma\) precisely using Nelder-Mead with 50 random restarts, converging to the global minimum of \(-\ell(\beta,\gamma)\).

← Overview 1–2 Data & model 3 Preprocessing 4 Likelihood 5 Grid search 6 MLE 7 GOF 8 CI 9 Validation 10–11 LHS & PRCC

Why minimize \(-\ell\) instead of maximizing \(\ell\)?

Raw likelihoods are products of tiny probabilities across 50 days: \(L = \prod_{t=1}^T P(y_t|\mu_t)\). Multiplying 50 small decimals gives numbers like \(10^{-30}\) — computers underflow to zero. Taking the log converts multiplication to addition. We then negate because standard optimizers are designed to minimize (find the lowest point), not maximize:

MLE objective
\[(\hat\beta,\,\hat\gamma) = \underset{\beta,\,\gamma\,>\,0}{\arg\min}\;\bigl[-\ell(\beta,\gamma)\bigr] \equiv \underset{\beta,\,\gamma\,>\,0}{\arg\max}\;\ell(\beta,\gamma)\]

Global vs local minima

\(-\ell\) landscape: global minimum (green ★ = true answer) vs local minima (traps)
Starting from the wrong location, an optimizer may converge to a local minimum and stop, thinking it has found the answer. The solution: run 50 restarts from random starting points.

How Nelder-Mead works

Nelder-Mead uses a simplex — in 2D this is a triangle. Each iteration, the triangle moves by doing one of four operations:

1. Reflect

Flip the worst vertex through the centroid of the remaining vertices

2. Expand

If reflection improved things, stretch further in that direction

3. Contract

If reflection failed, pull the worst vertex back toward the centroid

4. Shrink

If all else fails, collapse the whole triangle toward the best vertex
