1011 LHS, Sensitivity & MCMC

Uncertainty, Sensitivity & MCMC

Map full parameter uncertainty with Latin Hypercube Sampling, rank parameter importance with PRCC, and explore the posterior distribution with Metropolis-Hastings MCMC.

Step 10 — Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS)

LHS does not fit the model — it maps uncertainty. After obtaining the MLE estimates with CIs, LHS asks: across the entire plausible range of all parameters simultaneously, what is the full range of possible epidemic outcomes?

LHS sample size rule of thumb
\[n \geq \frac{4}{3}k^2 \quad (k = \text{number of parameters})\quad \text{For } k=5: n \geq 33. \text{ Standard: } n=500.\]

V29 — LHS vs random vs Sobol sampling

LHS — even coverage
One sample per bin — no gaps, no clusters.
Random — uneven clusters
Random clustering wastes samples in dense regions.
Sobol — ultra-even
Quasi-random sequence fills space most efficiently.

Step 11 — PRCC sensitivity analysis

PRCC (Partial Rank Correlation Coefficient) measures how strongly each input parameter drives the output (e.g. final epidemic size), after removing the influence of all other parameters.

PRCC calculation (5 steps)
\[\text{PRCC}(x_i, y \mid x_{-i}) = \text{Pearson}\!\left(\operatorname{rank}(x_i)_{\text{resid}},\; \operatorname{rank}(y)_{\text{resid}}\right)\]
|PRCC|StrengthAction
> 0.5StrongEstimate this parameter very precisely — top priority
0.2–0.5ModerateWorth collecting better data, but not critical
< 0.2NegligibleRough estimate sufficient — low priority

V33 — PRCC tornado chart

V33 — PRCC coefficients for final epidemic size. Red = positive driver, blue = negative driver.
\(\beta\) dominates (strong positive: higher transmission → larger outbreak). \(\gamma\) is the strongest negative driver (faster recovery → smaller outbreak). Detection delay is negligible — no need to measure it precisely.

MCMC — Metropolis-Hastings

Unlike LHS (which ignores the likelihood), MCMC samples proportional to the posterior. It spends more time in high-likelihood regions and produces a full posterior distribution for each parameter.

Acceptance probability
\[\alpha = \min\!\left(1,\; \frac{\pi(\theta^*\mid y)}{\pi(\theta_t\mid y)}\right)\]

If the proposed \(\theta^*\) has higher posterior than the current \(\theta_t\), always accept. If lower, accept with probability \(\alpha < 1\). This occasionally moves to worse regions — preventing the chain from getting stuck.
