7 Goodness of Fit

Goodness of Fit

Check whether the fitted model actually captures the data well — using residuals, Q-Q plots, ACF, and histogram diagnostics. Side-by-side good vs bad for each.

← Overview 1–2 Data & model 3 Preprocessing 4 Likelihood 5 Grid search 6 MLE 7 GOF 8 CI 9 Validation 10–11 LHS & PRCC

What are observed, predicted, and residuals?

Observed \(y_t\) = real daily farm case counts from the data. These never change.
Predicted \(\hat\mu_t\) = what the SIR ODE outputs using \(\hat\beta\) and \(\hat\gamma\). These are case counts, not \(\beta\) or \(\gamma\) themselves.
Residual \(e_t\) = the gap between them.

Pearson residual (NegBin)
\[e_t = \frac{y_t - \hat\mu_t}{\sqrt{\hat\mu_t\!\left(1 + \dfrac{\hat\mu_t}{k}\right)}}\]

If the model fits well, residuals should be approximately normal with mean 0, constant variance, and no autocorrelation.

V20 — Observed vs fitted epidemic curve

GOOD — line hugs the dots
Predicted curve passes through the observed dots — small random gaps only. Model captures timing, peak height, and decline.
BAD — line misses the peak
Predicted curve peaks too late and too low. Suggests \(\hat\beta\) is underestimated or \(\hat\gamma\) is overestimated.

V20b — Residuals vs fitted values

GOOD — random scatter around zero
No pattern — model captures all structure in the data.
BAD — arch pattern (systematic bias)
Arch = model misses nonlinearity (e.g. spatial heterogeneity or missing exposed class).

V22 — Q-Q plot of residuals

GOOD — points near diagonal
Residuals are approximately normally distributed.
BAD — heavy tails curve away
Residuals are skewed — try Negative Binomial or log-transform.

V21 — ACF of residuals

GOOD — all bars within blue bands
No temporal autocorrelation remains — model captured all time structure.
BAD — bars outside bands
High ACF at lag 7 = weekly reporting cycle not modelled. High ACF at lag 1 = model too slow to respond.
95% significance bands for ACF
\[\text{Band} = \pm\frac{1.96}{\sqrt{T}} \approx \pm 0.277 \text{ (for } T=50\text{)}\]
