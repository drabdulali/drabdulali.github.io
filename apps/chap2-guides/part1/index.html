Research Question

What are we trying to answer, and why does it matter?

"What ecological, environmental, and institutional risk factors predict
TAD persistence and reemergence at the admin-2 level?"

W1

WHAT are we predicting?

Outcome 1 — Persistence: Binary (0/1) — did disease recur within 6–24 months?

Outcome 2 — Reemergence: Time-to-event (months) — how long after ≥2 disease-free years until next outbreak?

W2

WHICH risk factors?

Livestock density · Tree cover · Wildlife proximity · Road density · Vaccination/culling coverage · GDP

W3

WHERE?

Pooled: 15 TADs across high-burden countries.

Case study: Romania (ASF) — 66,530 outbreaks, highest global burden, admin-2 communes.

W4

WHEN?

Pooled: 2005–2025 (20 years of WAHIS data).

Romania ASF: 2018–2024 (post-introduction, sufficient outbreak density).

Why This Matters

Scientific, methodological, and policy justifications

Scientific

Knowledge Gap

  • Most TAD research focuses on initial outbreak, not recurrence
  • Persistence and reemergence have different drivers than first introductions
  • Current models predict presence/absence — not temporal dynamics
  • Tests disease ecology principles on recurrence risk
Methodological

Innovation

  • Two complementary models: logistic regression (persistence) + Cox PH (reemergence)
  • Integrates four databases: WAHIS + GLW + FAOSTAT + remote sensing
  • Spatial epidemiology applied to longitudinal TAD data
  • Reproducible framework for other diseases
Policy

Real-World Impact

  • ASF costs Romania $100M+/year (culling, trade restrictions)
  • Enables risk-based surveillance — targeted, not blanket
  • Shifts from reactive to proactive prevention
  • 10–20% recurrence reduction saves millions

Model Formulas

The mathematical foundation of mixed effects models

Model 1 — Persistence

Mixed-Effects Logistic Regression

For binary outcome: did disease recur within 6–24 months?

General form
\[ \text{logit}(p_{ij}) = \beta_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{K} \beta_k x_{kij} + u_j \] // u_j ~ N(0, σ²) is the random effect for group j
Interpretation
  • β_k — log-odds change per 1 SD increase in predictor k
  • exp(β_k) — Odds Ratio (OR): how much more likely recurrence?
  • u_j — random intercept capturing unobserved clustering
  • OR > 1 → increases risk · OR < 1 → protective
Model 2 — Reemergence

Cox PH with Gamma Frailty

Time-to-event: months from disease-free period to next outbreak

General form
\[ h(t \mid x_i, b_j) = h_0(t) \cdot b_j \cdot \exp\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K} \beta_k x_{ki}\right) \] // b_j ~ Gamma(1/θ, 1/θ) is the frailty (random effect)
Interpretation
  • h₀(t) — baseline hazard: underlying reemergence rate
  • exp(β_k) — Hazard Ratio (HR): speed of reemergence
  • b_j — frailty term for unobserved disease/country clustering
  • HR > 1 → faster reemergence · HR < 1 → slower
Why Mixed Effects?

The full mixed effects structure

\[ \mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta} + \mathbf{Z}\mathbf{u} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \] // Y = outcomes | Xβ = fixed effects (your predictors) // Zu = random effects (disease, country, admin-2 clustering) // ε = residual error
✅ Why not simple logistic/Cox? Outbreaks within the same commune, country, or disease are correlated — ignoring this inflates false positives. Mixed effects correctly partition variance between within-group and between-group sources.

Key Definitions

Click each term to expand the full definition and justification

🔴 Persistence — Binary outcome, the "did it come back?" question ▼

Definition: A binary variable (0/1) indicating whether disease recurred at the same admin-2 unit within 6–24 months of the index outbreak.

Why 6 months lower bound? Eliminates surveillance artifacts — a new case within 6 months likely reflects incomplete control of the same wave, not a true new event.

Why 24 months upper bound? Beyond 24 months, an area transitions to the "reemergence" definition (≥2 disease-free years). The two outcomes are mutually exclusive by design.

Persistenceij = 1 if outbreak occurs at unit j within [6, 24] months of event i
Persistenceij = 0 otherwise (disease-free or censored)
🟣 Reemergence — Time-to-event outcome, the "when did it come back?" question ▼

Definition: The time (in months) from the start of a disease-free period (≥2 consecutive years with zero reported outbreaks) until the next confirmed outbreak at the same admin-2 unit.

Why ≥2 years for disease-free? Two years ensures the area has cleared infection and susceptible population has rebuilt. Shorter periods cannot distinguish reintroduction from persistent low-level transmission.

Right-censoring: Units that do not experience reemergence by study end (2024) are censored — their time-to-event is known to be at least the follow-up period. Cox PH handles censoring correctly, unlike logistic regression.

Tj = time from disease-free start → reemergence event at unit j
δj = 1 if reemergence observed; δj = 0 if censored
🔵 Z-Scoring — Why all predictors are standardised before modelling ▼

Definition: Each continuous predictor x is transformed to z = (x − μ) / σ, where μ is the mean and σ the standard deviation across all observations.

Why? Predictors are on completely different scales: livestock density (animals/km²), GDP (USD), road density (km/km²). Without z-scoring, coefficients cannot be compared — a "1-unit" change means something different for each variable.

After z-scoring: A β of 0.5 means "a 1 SD increase in this predictor is associated with..." — directly comparable across all predictors. This also improves numerical stability of model fitting.

\[ z_{kj} = \frac{x_{kj} - \bar{x}_k}{s_k} \]
// Result: all predictors have mean=0, SD=1
🟡 Admin-2 Level — Why commune/county is the right spatial scale ▼

Definition: Admin-2 refers to the second administrative division — in Romania these are communes (~3,181 total), roughly equivalent to townships or small counties.

Why not farm-level? WAHIS does not provide exact GPS coordinates (privacy). Farm-level analysis is impossible with available data.

Why not admin-1 (county)? Too coarse — misses within-province heterogeneity in land use, livestock density, and road infrastructure that drives transmission differences.

Why admin-2 is optimal: It matches the veterinary control unit, is the scale at which culling/vaccination decisions are made, and provides adequate sample size (537 affected communes).

Study Design at a Glance

Every major design decision with its justification — ready for exam or peer review

Component Decision Justification
Study Type Observational, retrospective cohort with spatial epidemiological design Can't randomize disease exposure. Uses existing 20-yr WAHIS records. Disease transmission is inherently spatial.
Outcome 1 Binary — recurrence within 6–24 months Matches policy question (yes/no recurring risk). 6–24 month window avoids surveillance artifacts and overlaps with reemergence.
Outcome 2 Time-to-event — months to reemergence after ≥2 disease-free years Captures temporal dynamics. Handles right-censoring. ≥2 years distinguishes true reintroduction from persistence.
Statistical Model 1 Mixed-effects logistic regression Binary outcome → logistic. Random effects → clustering by disease/country/admin-2. Odds ratios are policy-relevant.
Statistical Model 2 Cox PH with gamma frailty Time-to-event standard. Frailty = random effects for clustering. Correctly handles censored observations.
Unit of Analysis Admin-2 (commune/county) WAHIS reports at admin-2. Matches veterinary control units. Best balance of spatial resolution and sample size.
Study Location Pooled: 15 TADs in high-burden countries + case study: Romania (ASF) Pooled tests generalizability. Romania: highest ASF burden (66K outbreaks), complete data, represents Europe.
Study Period Pooled: 2005–2025 · Romania: 2018–2024 20 years = comprehensive WAHIS coverage. Romania: post-introduction + sufficient outbreak density from 2018.
Sample Size 1,976 outbreaks · 537 admin-2 units · 6 years 404 EPV (events per variable) — 40× higher than the 10 EPV minimum. Extremely stable estimates, minimal overfitting risk.
Variable Selection Univariate screening + expert knowledge + correlation analysis Univariate: p<0.25 threshold. Expert: biological plausibility. Correlation: r>0.7 → remove redundant. Balances stats with mechanism.
Validation 70/30 train-test split + 10-fold CV Stratified split ensures balanced outcomes. 10-fold CV detects overfitting within training set.

Expected Contributions

What this work adds to science, methods, and policy

🔬

Scientific

  • First multi-TAD analysis of recurrence risk factors (not just initial occurrence)
  • Tests generalizability of disease ecology principles across 15 TADs
  • Distinguishes persistence from reemergence as mechanistically separate processes
  • Connects spatial epidemiology with temporal disease dynamics
⚙️

Methodological

  • Dual-model framework: logistic (persistence) + Cox PH (reemergence)
  • Reproducible pipeline integrating 4 databases
  • Elastic net regularisation for high-dimensional predictor selection
  • Transferable framework to other diseases and countries
🏛️

Policy

  • Risk maps identifying high-reemergence communes → targeted surveillance
  • Evidence base for proactive prevention over reactive response
  • Quantifies relative importance of ecological vs institutional factors
  • Framework for cost-effective TAD control resource allocation

One-sentence summary for exam/viva:
This study uses mixed-effects logistic regression and Cox proportional hazards with gamma frailty to identify ecological, environmental, and institutional predictors of TAD persistence (binary, 6–24 months) and reemergence (time-to-event, after ≥2 disease-free years), using Romania ASF as a case study (1,976 outbreaks, 537 communes, 2018–2024) within a broader 15-TAD pooled analysis.
