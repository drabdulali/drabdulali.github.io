Model Equations
M1
Mixed-Effects Logistic Regression — Persistence
Binary outcome: 0/1 recurrence within 6–24 months
▼

Input

Binary persistence (0/1) + z-scored predictors

Output

Odds Ratios (OR = \(e^\beta\)) with 95% CI

Why

Binary outcome requires logistic link. Mixed effects handles clustering across disease/country/admin-2.

Logistic regression with nested random effects
\[ \log\!\left(\frac{P_{ij}}{1 - P_{ij}}\right) = \underbrace{\beta_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{K} \beta_k z_{kij}}_{\text{fixed effects}} + \underbrace{u_{\text{disease}} + u_{\text{country}} + u_{\text{admin2}}}_{\text{random effects}} \]
  • \(P_{ij}\) = probability of persistence for outbreak \(i\) in group \(j\)
  • \(z_{kij}\) = z-scored predictor \(k\) — standardised so all \(\beta_k\) are comparable
  • \(\beta_k\) = log-odds coefficient; convert via \(\text{OR}_k = e^{\beta_k}\)
  • \(u_j \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)\) = random intercept absorbing between-group variation
Why random effects are essential Without \(u_j\), standard errors are underestimated, p-values are too small, and you get false positives. The random intercepts absorb systematic between-group differences (e.g. ASF persists more than FMD at baseline), leaving the \(\beta_k\) to cleanly estimate predictor effects.
M2
Cox Proportional Hazards with Gamma Frailty — Reemergence
Time-to-event: months from disease-free period to next outbreak
▼

Input

Survival time (months) + event indicator (1 = reemerged, 0 = censored)

Output

Hazard Ratios (HR = \(e^\beta\)) — already in exp(coef) column of summary

Why Cox

Handles right-censoring. Frailty is a multiplicative random effect for clustering. No distributional assumption on \(h_0(t)\).

Cox PH with gamma frailty
\[ h(t \mid \mathbf{z}_i,\, b_j) = h_0(t) \cdot b_j \cdot \exp\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K} \beta_k z_{ki}\right) \]

\(h_0(t)\) = baseline hazard  ·  \(b_j \sim \text{Gamma}(1/\theta,\,1/\theta)\) = frailty  ·  \(\text{HR}_k = e^{\beta_k}\)

General mixed model form
\[ \mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta} + \mathbf{Z}\mathbf{u} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \]

\(\mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta}\) = fixed effects  ·  \(\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{u}\) = random effects  ·  \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\) = residual

Key Definitions
D1
Persistence — Definition and Operationalisation
Binary outcome: the "did it come back?" question
▼

Definition: A binary variable indicating whether disease recurred at the same admin-2 unit within 6–24 months of the index outbreak.

\[ \text{Persistence}_{ij} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{outbreak at unit } j \text{ within } [6,\,24] \text{ months of event } i \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]
Why 6-month lower bound? A new case within 6 months likely reflects incomplete control of the same wave, not a true new event. Removing <6 months eliminates surveillance artifacts.
Why 24-month upper bound? Beyond 24 months an area transitions to the reemergence definition (≥2 disease-free years). The two outcomes are mutually exclusive by design.
D2
Reemergence — Definition and Operationalisation
Time-to-event: the "when did it come back?" question
▼

Definition: Time (months) from the start of a disease-free period (≥2 consecutive years) until the next confirmed outbreak at the same admin-2 unit.

\[ T_j = \text{months from disease-free start} \;\to\; \text{reemergence at unit } j \] \[ \delta_j = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{reemergence observed before study end} \\ 0 & \text{right-censored (no event by 2024)} \end{cases} \]
Why ≥2 years for disease-free? Ensures the area has cleared infection and the susceptible population has rebuilt. Shorter periods cannot distinguish reintroduction from persistent low-level transmission. The Cox model correctly handles all censored observations — their follow-up time contributes to the likelihood without bias.
D3
Z-Scoring — Why All Predictors Are Standardised
Enables direct comparison of coefficients across different measurement scales
▼
Z-score transformation
\[ z_{kj} = \frac{x_{kj} - \bar{x}_k}{s_k} \]

Result: every predictor has mean = 0, SD = 1 across all observations

Why this matters Predictors are on incomparable scales: livestock density (animals/km²), GDP (USD), road density (km/km²). Without z-scoring a "1-unit" change means something entirely different for each variable. After z-scoring, \(\beta_k = 0.5\) always means "a 1-SD increase in predictor \(k\) is associated with..." — all coefficients are directly comparable in magnitude.

Viva / exam answer This study uses mixed-effects logistic regression and Cox proportional hazards with gamma frailty to identify ecological, environmental, and institutional predictors of TAD persistence (binary, 6–24 months) and reemergence (time-to-event, after ≥2 disease-free years), using Romania ASF as a case study (1,976 outbreaks, 537 communes, 2018–2024) within a broader 15-TAD pooled analysis — with three-level nested random effects (disease / country / admin-2) to correctly account for clustering.
