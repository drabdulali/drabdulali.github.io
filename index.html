About

Where ecology meets epidemiology

As a PhD researcher in infectious disease ecology, I investigate the intricate interactions between pathogens, hosts, and their environments. My work integrates ecological theory with mathematical modeling to understand how diseases emerge, spread, and can be controlled.

My current research focuses on Transboundary Animal Diseases — exploring their past, present, and future — contributing to both fundamental science and global public health preparedness through the One Health approach.

Research

Current Research Areas

Investigating fundamental questions at the frontier of disease ecology and epidemiology.

01

Disease Transmission Networks

Analyzing how contact patterns and network structure shape the spread of infectious diseases — using network theory to identify superspreaders and optimize interventions.

02

Host-Pathogen Coevolution

Studying the evolutionary dynamics between hosts and pathogens — virulence evolution, resistance mechanisms, and the ecological factors shaping these relationships.

03

Environmental Drivers of Disease

Investigating how climate change, habitat fragmentation, and anthropogenic disturbance influence disease emergence and transmission across ecological communities.

04

Zoonotic Disease Spillover

Examining the ecological and evolutionary factors that facilitate pathogen spillover from wildlife to humans, with implications for pandemic preparedness and One Health.

05

Mathematical Epidemiology

Developing and analyzing models to understand epidemic dynamics, predict outbreak trajectories, and evaluate the effectiveness of public health interventions.

06

Wildlife Disease Surveillance

Designing optimal surveillance strategies for detecting and monitoring infectious diseases in wildlife populations, integrating field methods with statistical modeling.
